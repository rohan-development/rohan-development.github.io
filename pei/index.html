July 6, 2026

Day 14: Grand Sault

Today was a short day, culminating in a crazy series of hills at the end. We left Edmundston at 7:15, local time after a generous breakfast provided by Denis and Lynne. The first 50 KM were pretty relaxed, however when we got close to our destination, we saw some of the biggest hills we'd ever seen. To make matters worse, they were rolling hills, so we just went up and down several times. We arrived at our warmshowers for the night, whcih is just outside of Grand Sault, NB. More updates and pictures to come tonight :)

July 5, 2026

Day 13: Edmundston

Today was a long day, but it ended on a very high note. We left Riviere-du-Loup at around 7 and headed for Edmundston. It was extremely cold, even after we put on all our clothes. We biked on, stopping at a gas station to make some Uncle Ben's rice with our gasoline-powered stove. By this point, we realized that we had spent too long there, and were on track to get to Edmundston at 5 pm due to the time change (ahead one hour). We arrived in Edmundston at 6:30 pm, AST, with the plan to shower at a fire station again. Unfortunately, the Edmundston fire department did not have a shower, so that was out. We spent the next hour searching for a place to shower, before we eventually cut our losses and began to look for a place to stay. It was at this point that our fortunes took a complete 180. After many "no's," one couple finally allowed us to camp in their backyard. Denis and Lynne took us in, eventually setting us up with an air mattress in their garage, let us shower inside, invited us in to use their kitchen to make dinner, and even gave us ice cream sandwiches. We then talked for about an hour, after we met their son Mark. They are truly lovely people, and made our day infinitely better. Tomorrow, we head for Grand Sault!

Hard to see, but crossing into NB!

July 4, 2026

Day 12: Riviere-du-Loup

Today started off mildly upsetting, but was able to redeem itself at the end. Yesterday, we were unable to find jam for our peanut butter and jam sandwiches, so we chose to buy marmalade instead, thinking it would be fine. Unfortunately, the two did not mix and they required much water to go down. We left La Pocatière this morning at 7:30, and set off on road 132. We followed it for about 80 KM until we reached Riviere-du-Loup (pretty quickly because we dreaded stopping to eat). We finished the sandwiches before Riviere du loup, and bought a bunch of instant noodles, fruit, and supplies for tomorrow. We ate the instant noodles in the parking lot, spending a total of 2 hours there. Finally, we left Riviere du Loup and followed a rail trail towards Edmundston, NB. The trail was essentially uphill for all 20 KM we took it, but eventually we arrived at a beautiful campsite on the side of the trail. We were able to bathe in the river. Overall, a pretty good day!

July 3, 2026

Day 11: La Pocatière

Today was a long, hot day. Many bottles of water were consumed. We stopped for lunch along the Saint Lawrence, which was super nice. We also had a 30 KM/H tailwind, which propelled us towards La Pocatière; we were cruising between 30 and 40 KM/H for a lot of the day. When we arrived at La Pocatière, we didn't know if we would get a shower because we were staying in someone's backyard, so we stopped by a local fire station to ask. The guy (Mattias) was super nice, and let us take a shower in the station's showers. We an absolute dinner of champions, clocking in with instant ramen, oreos, powdered milk, and tea.

Day 10: Quebec City

Day 9: Lyster

Today was probably the hottest day we had so far. We each drank several litres of water, while sweating buckets. The ride itself was extremely flat, and entirely on rail trails. Overall a pretty average day. We were welcomed in Lyster by Mariette and Inree, a local couple, which required us to brush up on our French! We did not take many pictures today :(

June 30, 2026

Day 8: Richmond

Today was a great day. We start strong again with another 12 eggs, scrambled of course. We biked through some super nice paved trails again before getting into the countryside. Eventually, the paved trails were no more, and we found ourselves on a dirt road. While it was slightly tougher, and uphil, we managed to make it to Richmond. We hit 60 KM/H on our descent into Richmond, and saw literal smoke coming off Rohan's brakes when we stopped (no joke, we really did). We were welcomed by Guy, who lives in Richmond, and is an amazing cook. We had some amazing deer meat, which he hunted himself, as well as greens, potatoes, and to top it off, some excellent panacota. At the end of meal, as is tradition in Quebec, we had a plate of fresh cheese.

Day 7: Granby

Today was a long day. We started strong with 12 scrambled eggs, and some turkey bacon. After Theo's bike trouble yesterday, we left Montreal at around 10:30, and headed for Granby. We are starting to get to the point where things are starting to hurt, but the end is still quite far. In Granby, we are staying with Andre, a warmshowers host with a beautiful mini house in his backyard. Tomorrow, we are aiming for Richmond.

June 28, 2026

Day 6: Montreal

Yesterday we chose to make it all the way to Montreal. We were forced to make a pitstop at McDonald’s to make all our sandwiches for the day which set us back an hour. While we were eating our sandwiches (after passing the Ontario/Quebec border), we were welcomed into a Mel and Willem's backyard where we shared lovely stories and they provided us with plenty of food. They were absolutely lovely people, and genuinely interested in our journey! Unfortunately, this did set us back another hour (though it was totally worth it). With just about 65 km left, the rain began to come down hard (it was a torrential downpour). While putting the tent in a garbage bag, Theo’s bike fell and his pannier rack broke. Luckily, nothing important got wet, and we were able to do laundry and dry off when we arrived in Rohan’s apartment in Montreal. The rain finally stopped and we made it to Montreal by 7:15 pm with spirits at an all time low. On Monday (today now) we go to bike shops to get a new pannier rack.

Day 5: Cornwall

Day 5 was smooth sailing for the most part (our legs are taking a toll)... A late start at 9 am meant we were playing catch up all day, but thankfully the weather gods were on our side once again. Day 4: Brockville

Today was an extremely lucky day. We woke up at 5:30 initially, but we pushed the wakeup by an hour since it was pouring rain, in hopes of dodging the rain. The morning started with egg and english muffin sandwiches from Richard, which were super good and kept us fueled! When we passed through Kingston we met up with our friend Evan. Throughout the day, we encountered some mild rain, but mostly stayed dry. Much of today was biked on a trail beside the Thousand Islands Parkway, which was awesome. Yesterday's cheese and turkey sandwiches were a success and we stopped along the trail on a nice rocky outcrop to eat the second batch. We arrived in Brockville around 3 pm, where we were greeted by our Warmshower hosts Jacky and Ralf. They are extremely kind, they provided us with showers, dinner, and several hours of conversations, including their cycling adventures across the world! Shortly after our arrival it began to pour rain.

We plan to change up the diet tomorrow with chicken and cheese sandwiches so we will be sure to give a rating!
